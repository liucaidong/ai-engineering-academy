# M15 Claim-to-Source Review — Agent Security, Privacy and Governance

> Review date: 2026-11-10  
> Scope: 21 lesson-level source occurrences, 20 unique URLs, five lessons.  
> Result: 21/21 source occurrences matched to the claims they support; current NIST/OWASP/EU/ISO version status and terminology were reconciled.

## Key findings and qualifications

1. **STRIDE and ATLAS:** STRIDE defines six general threat categories; MITRE ATLAS is a separate AI adversarial TTP knowledge base. The course’s STRIDE↔ATLAS crosswalk is an instructional heuristic, not an official Microsoft/MITRE mapping. The 1–5 risk score and severity thresholds are local prioritization rules, not NIST/OWASP probability formulas.
2. **AI RMF status:** NIST AI RMF 1.0 is the current published baseline and is voluntary; NIST has started revising it. NIST AI 600-1 is a GenAI-specific companion profile. The Playbook is voluntary suggested actions, not a checklist, and will be updated after the RMF revision.
3. **Privacy Framework:** NIST Privacy Framework 1.0 remains the published final version; 1.1 is an Initial Public Draft and must not be described as final.
4. **SP 800-53:** Release 5.2.0 was announced 2025-08-27. The release updates selected controls/discussion but does not change SP 800-53B control baselines; use CPRT for the actual catalog.
5. **ABAC and Zero Trust:** SP 800-162 Update 2 is the current final ABAC source. NIST SP 800-207 uses Policy Engine (PE), Policy Administrator (PA) and Policy Enforcement Point (PEP); generic PDP/PEP is related but not identical terminology.
6. **Taint and egress:** Untrusted web/email/document/repository/OCR content is data, not an instruction source. Unknown provenance, unknown data labels and unknown destinations must fail closed. Even recognized user instructions require Host policy approval before tool execution.
7. **Approval integrity:** Approval binds principal, authenticated approver, normalized action digest, expiry and nonce. The example now consumes nonce atomically within one process; production needs a durable shared uniqueness constraint, trusted approval service, key management, trusted time and cross-language canonicalization (e.g. RFC 8785).
8. **Human oversight:** The risk-tier thresholds are organization-defined examples, not thresholds mandated by NIST or the EU AI Act. The EU AI Act consolidated text dated 2026-07-27 includes certain recruitment/candidate-selection use cases in Annex III; classification depends on intended purpose and statutory conditions. Following the AI Omnibus entering into force on 2026-07-27, Annex III high-risk rules apply from 2027-12-02 and Annex I product-related high-risk rules from 2028-08-02; general applicability began 2026-08-02. Obtain legal review.
9. **ISO/IEC 42001:** The 2023 standard specifies an organizational AI Management System; it is not a product safety certification.
10. **Incident response:** NIST SP 800-61 Rev. 3 is the 2025 final guidance aligned with CSF 2.0. The course’s detect/triage/contain/preserve/revoke/quarantine/scope/recover/notify/review flow is an exercise checklist, not a universal strictly serial NIST state machine.
11. **Testing references:** OWASP WSTG v4.2 is the pinned stable web-security testing guide; it is not Agent-specific. OWASP GenAI LLM Top 10 2026 and Agentic Applications Top 10 2026 are risk frameworks, not regulations or certifications. MITRE’s OpenClaw investigation is a case study, not a frequency dataset.
12. **Teaching code limitations:** Taint labels are caller-supplied; no automatic content classification. The HMAC demo uses Python JSON serialization, not cross-language JCS; its nonce store is process-local. The oversight tiers and incident stages are illustrative. The sample does not integrate a real IAM, KMS, SIEM, container runtime, legal workflow or production red-team environment.
