# M19 Claim-to-Source Review — AI Product Engineering and Delivery

> Review date: 2026-11-10  
> Scope: 20 lesson-level source occurrences, 15 unique URLs, five lessons.  
> Result: all 20 occurrences matched to current primary/official sources; source links were updated to claim-specific pages.

## Findings and qualifications

1. **Product discovery:** Product Talk's Opportunity Solution Tree connects desired outcomes, customer opportunities, candidate solutions and assumption tests. It is a discovery/learning method, not proof of demand or product-market fit. User interviews and behavior data require sampling and bias controls.
2. **Human-centered AI:** Stanford HAI defines human-centered AI in terms of human needs, values and well-being. Google PAIR Guidebook v2 covers user needs, success, mental models, trust, feedback/control and graceful failure. These are design references, not regulatory standards.
3. **AI UX guidelines:** Microsoft HAX Toolkit provides 18 evidence-based guidelines across four interaction stages and synthesizes research associated with the CHI 2019 paper. Guidelines should be selected and tested against context, risk, accessibility and user research; they are not a compliance checklist.
4. **NIST AI RMF:** AI RMF 1.0 is voluntary and NIST states it is being revised. The Playbook contains suggested actions, is not a checklist, and will be updated after the framework revision. Do not describe it as mandatory law or a finalized updated framework.
5. **Human approval:** Binding an approval to actor/resource/operation/arguments digest/policy version/expiry/idempotency key prevents stale or altered approval data from being reused across different actions, but field equality alone does not stop replay. A production service must atomically consume a nonce/approval state, authenticate the approver, reauthorize at execution and retain audit evidence.
6. **Unit economics:** FinOps Unit Economics explicitly connects technology spend and use to business value and distinguishes resource-efficiency metrics from business-unit metrics. Cost per successful task must use independently verified successful tasks as denominator. All amounts need one currency, Decimal/fixed-point arithmetic, a price effective date/version and explicit allocation of retries, human review and shared costs.
7. **Pricing:** OpenAI's official pricing page is a live, time-sensitive source, not a stable textbook constant. Store model, region, modality, token class, cache treatment, price unit, currency, discount and effective timestamp. The course's numeric examples are illustrative and must not be mistaken for current price quotes.
8. **A/B experiment trustworthiness:** Kohavi, Tang and Xu's 2020 Cambridge University Press book covers randomization units, A/A tests, SRM, interference and statistical pitfalls. A two-arm SRM chi-square test has one degree of freedom; its p-value diagnoses allocation/data integrity, not treatment effect.
9. **Multiple metrics:** Microsoft's 2026 Experimentation Platform article reports its empirical comparison of p-value aggregation methods on A/A scorecards and its use of Benjamini–Hochberg. BH controls false discovery rate under applicable assumptions; arbitrary metric dependence, irrelevant metrics, selection after seeing outcomes and unregistered hypotheses still threaten interpretation. Predefine primary and guardrail metrics and document metric relevance.
10. **Release lifecycle:** DORA's Continuous Delivery capability emphasizes deployable software, low-risk releases, test/deployment automation and fast feedback. The teaching manifest includes code/image/runtime/model/Prompt/tool/API/index/dataset/evaluation/policy/feature-flag versions, but does not prove production rollback compatibility. Database/index migrations, model/provider contracts, data recoverability, signatures, canary evidence and approvals must be checked by the actual release controller.
11. **Teaching code limits:** Python scripts use synthetic data and standard-library calculations. They do not run real customer discovery, authenticate or execute approvals, reconcile provider invoices, run a production experimentation platform, or deploy/rollback a real service.
