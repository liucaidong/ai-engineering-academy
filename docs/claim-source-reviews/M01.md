# M01 Claim-to-Source Review — AI, Machine Learning and LLM Foundations

> Review date: 2026-11-10. Scope: 15 lesson-level source occurrences across five lessons.

## Findings and qualifications

- OECD's Recommendation on AI (OECD/LEGAL/0449) is in force and was amended in 2023 and 2024. Its current AI-system definition covers machine-based inference from inputs to predictions, content, recommendations or decisions and recognizes varying autonomy/adaptiveness after deployment. It is a policy recommendation, not a universal technical taxonomy.
- The OECD explanatory memorandum endpoint returned HTTP 403 to the local crawler; its source identity and core definition were corroborated against the in-force OECD legal instrument and the OECD 2026 responsible-AI glossary. The crawler result is not represented as HTTP 200.
- NIST AI RMF 1.0 is intended for voluntary use and NIST says it is being revised. The Playbook is voluntary suggested action, not a mandatory checklist.
- Google ML Crash Course, ISLR and scikit-learn support introductory learning concepts and practical supervised-learning examples; they are pedagogical/technical references with different scope, not interchangeable authorities.
- GPT-3 and GPT-4 reports are historical technical research sources. GPT-3's few-shot results are tied to its 2020 benchmarks and methodology. GPT-4's report explicitly withholds architecture size, hardware, training compute and dataset-construction details; the course must not infer them.
- OWASP GenAI LLM Top 10 2026 is a risk taxonomy, not a complete threat model or a certification standard.
- The lesson's model/application/system decomposition is an engineering synthesis, not a quotation from one source.
