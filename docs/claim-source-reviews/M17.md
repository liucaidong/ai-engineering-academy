# M17 Claim-to-Source Review — Evaluation, Judge Calibration and AI Observability

> Review date: 2026-11-10  
> Scope: 21 lesson-level source occurrences, 19 unique URLs, five lessons.  
> Result: 21/21 source occurrences matched to their claims; statistical estimators, evaluator limitations, telemetry privacy, semantic-convention migration and framework maturity were reconciled.

## Key findings and qualifications

1. **HELM:** The 2022 HELM paper proposes holistic evaluation across scenarios and dimensions; the Stanford dashboard is dynamic. Any reported HELM result must identify suite, scenario, model, metrics and retrieval/version date.
2. **Eval harness:** Anthropic’s eval guide uses task, trial, grader, transcript and outcome concepts. The local course harness validates a task contract and outcome; it does not run an isolated agent, enforce sandboxing or establish grader reliability.
3. **Datasheet governance:** The Datasheets for Datasets preprint appeared in 2018 and the final paper was published in Communications of the ACM in 2021. Dataset documentation improves provenance and intended-use transparency; it does not prove representativeness, label correctness or freedom from leakage.
4. **pass@k distinction:** The IID Bernoulli approximation is `1−(1−p)^k`; pass^k is `p^k`. HumanEval uses the finite-sample unbiased estimator `1−C(n−c,k)/C(n,k)` from n samples with c correct. These are different estimands/assumptions and must not be conflated. The code now implements both.
5. **Statistical uncertainty:** Wilson intervals are appropriate for binomial proportions; paired bootstrap resamples task-level paired deltas. Repeated trials nested within a task are not independent observations; cluster/paired design is required. Confidence intervals do not correct dataset contamination, judge bias or selection effects.
6. **LLM Judge:** MT-Bench/Chatbot Arena reports useful agreement with human preferences while documenting position, verbosity and self-enhancement biases. G-Eval uses CoT/form-filling rubrics. Neither paper makes LLM scores objective truth; calibrate on human labels and retain abstain/escalation paths.
7. **ARES:** ARES evaluates context relevance, answer faithfulness and answer relevance using synthetic data and lightweight judges, then applies Prediction-Powered Inference with a small human-labeled calibration set. It is not fully human-free evaluation.
8. **Prometheus evaluator:** The rubric/reference-guided evaluator paper is ICLR 2024 (arXiv 2023). Its reported alignment is bounded by training/evaluation datasets, model and rubric distribution; do not confuse this paper with the Prometheus monitoring system.
9. **NIST AI RMF:** AI RMF 1.0 is the current published voluntary baseline organized around Govern/Map/Measure/Manage; NIST has started revising it. The Playbook is voluntary suggested actions, not a mandatory checklist, and will be updated after the RMF revision.
10. **OpenTelemetry GenAI:** Core semantic-conventions v1.42.0 deprecates the old `gen_ai.*` attributes, metrics, events and spans in that repository and moves their active definitions to the dedicated `semantic-conventions-genai` repository. The dedicated GenAI conventions remain Development, not Stable. Pin the dedicated schema URL and instrumentation/convention versions; do not treat the old core-repository attributes as current normative definitions.
11. **Telemetry privacy and cardinality:** Span attributes should be allowlisted; unknown/custom content attributes may contain PII or secrets. Metric labels must be low-cardinality; user_id, request_id, document_id, prompts and arbitrary tool arguments belong nowhere in Prometheus labels. Trace context is correlation metadata, not authorization.
12. **SLO and drift:** Google SRE SLO/error-budget guidance is service reliability practice, not an AI quality certification. Evidently drift metrics are diagnostic signals; distribution drift alone does not prove quality degradation or justify automatic retraining. Safety hard constraints must remain separate from aggregate error budgets.
13. **Teaching code limits:** The statistical data are synthetic; bootstrap is a simple task-level resampler; judge calibration uses boolean labels; telemetry allowlists do not connect to an OTel SDK/Collector; SLO calculations omit low-traffic/window-alignment nuances. No benchmark or production quality guarantee is implied.
